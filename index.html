01 The Bastar plateau

Straddling southern Chhattisgarh, this forested plateau has been the movement's core base since the 1980s and remains the site of most current operations on both sides.

02 The tri-junction belt

Where Chhattisgarh, Odisha and Maharashtra meet, terrain and weak road access historically made this the hardest ground for security forces to hold year-round.

03 The eastern corridor

Jharkhand and western Odisha carry the MCC lineage of the movement — a different organisational history from the PWG-descended southern base, still visible in local cadre structure.

04 Retreat from the north

Districts in Bihar, eastern Uttar Pradesh and West Bengal that were active through the 1990s and 2000s have been reclassified as low-activity or dropped from the affected list entirely over the past decade.

RECORD · 1967–PRESENT

Nearly six decades, in phases.

Each phase marks a shift in leadership, geography or method — not a clean break. Scroll to move through the record.

1967

Naxalbari

A peasant uprising in a village in West Bengal's Darjeeling district gives the movement its name, splitting from the mainstream Communist Party of India (Marxist).

1969–72

CPI(ML) and early collapse

The Communist Party of India (Marxist-Leninist) forms and leads an armed uprising; state suppression and internal splits fragment the movement within a few years.

1980

People's War Group

Formed in Andhra Pradesh, PWG rebuilds a base in the forested belt of Telangana and moves the movement's centre of gravity toward central India.

1969–90s

Maoist Communist Centre

Operating separately out of Bihar and later Jharkhand, the MCC builds a parallel base among landless and tribal communities in the eastern belt.

2004

Merger: CPI (Maoist)

PWG and MCC merge to form the Communist Party of India (Maoist), unifying the eastern and southern bases into a single organisation and command structure.

2006–10

Red Corridor peak

The government's own assessments describe the insurgency as the country's foremost internal security concern; the affected-district list reaches its widest extent.

2009–11

Operation Green Hunt

Coordinated central and state paramilitary operations push into core forest districts, marking a shift toward sustained counter-insurgency deployment.

2010–18

Contraction and fortification

Camps, road-building and mobile-network expansion into forest interiors gradually shrink the movement's uncontested territory, concentrated increasingly around the Bastar plateau.

2019–24

Leadership losses, narrower base

A string of encounters and surrenders reduces senior cadre strength; the affected-district count is repeatedly revised downward in official reporting.

2025–26

Present phase

Remaining activity is concentrated in a small number of core districts on the Chhattisgarh–Odisha–Maharashtra tri-junction; the state has set public timelines for ending the conflict.

METHODOLOGY

How the archive is built.

Three checks apply to every entry before it's published.

01 / SOURCE

Primary before secondary

Government reports, court records and field visits are weighed above news aggregation. Where only secondary sources exist, that's noted on the entry.

02 / DATE-STAMP

Every figure carries a date

Affected-district counts, casualty figures and cadre-strength estimates change often. We stamp the reporting date rather than presenting a single current number.

03 / VERIFICATION

Claims are cross-checked

Key claims are checked against multiple reliable sources wherever possible. Conflicting figures, reporting gaps and important uncertainties are identified rather than presented as settled fact.

Follow new entries and data updates.

A concise update when new reporting, data or timeline revisions are published.
